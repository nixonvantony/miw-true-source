# CANDIDATE TRAPS AND MISSING POINTS

Package: general-average · as_at: 2026-08-13

## Rule A vs Rule C — what is GA act vs what is allowed in adjustment

**Object ID:** `TRAP-RULE-A-C`

### Wrong / incomplete form
'All losses during casualty are general average.'

### Why it is wrong
Rule A requires **extraordinary sacrifice/expense intentionally and reasonably made for common safety**. Rule C excludes losses that are **consequence of delay** (e.g. market loss, deterioration from delay).

### Correct structure
Apply Rule A test (extraordinary + intentional + reasonable + common safety); exclude Rule C losses (delay consequences).

## Rule D — treating fault as a bar to GA contribution

**Object ID:** `TRAP-RULE-D-FAULT`

*Corrected 2026-08-15 (TSCR-6) against the CMI primary text. This object previously stated the inverse of Rule D — that a party at fault cannot claim contribution, and that the Rule Paramount bars GA on fault. Both propositions were wrong.*

### Wrong / incomplete form
'A party at fault cannot claim contribution in general average' — or, in its Rule-Paramount dress, 'the Rule Paramount bars GA where the party claiming was at fault.'

### Why it is wrong
Rule D says the opposite: rights to contribution **shall not be affected**, though the event may have been due to the fault of one of the parties, *"but this shall not prejudice any remedies or defences which may be open against or to that party in respect of such fault"* (`YAR-D`). Rule D **preserves** the GA right and **relocates** the consequence of fault out of the adjustment and into remedies and defences under the applicable law. The adjustment proceeds; the fault is fought separately.

The Rule Paramount (`YAR-PARAMOUNT`) is a **reasonableness gate**, not a fault gate: *"In no case shall there be any allowance for sacrifice or expenditure unless reasonably made or incurred."* It asks whether the sacrifice or expenditure was reasonable — not who caused the casualty. Attributing a fault bar to it is a misattribution, not an overstatement.

### Correct structure
1. Apply Rule D: fault does **not** defeat the right to contribution; the adjustment proceeds.
2. Apply the Rule Paramount separately: allow nothing unless **reasonably made or incurred**.
3. Deal with fault where Rule D puts it — in remedies and defences under the applicable law and contract.

### The reason this matters
Any bar on a shipowner at fault recovering in GA comes from the **applicable law**, not from the Rules — which is precisely why the **New Jason Clause** exists. Teach the fault bar as a YAR rule and the explanation of that clause collapses.

### The opposite error
Do not over-correct into 'GA is payable regardless of fault.' Rule D preserves the *right to contribution*; it expressly leaves the fault-based remedies and defences intact.

## GA vs particular average confusion

**Object ID:** `TRAP-GA-PA`

### Wrong / incomplete form
'All damage in casualty is general average.'

### Why it is wrong
Particular average = loss/damage borne by owner of damaged property (not shared). GA = shared proportionally by all interests (ship, cargo, freight).

### Correct structure
Distinguish GA (shared) from PA (borne by owner).

## Salvage in GA — YAR Rule VI vs Art. 14/SCOPIC

**Object ID:** `TRAP-SALVAGE-GA`

### Wrong / incomplete form
'Salvage remuneration (Art. 14/SCOPIC) is allowed in GA.'

### Why it is wrong
YAR 2016 Rule VI: salvage payments (including special compensation under Art. 14/SCOPIC) are **excluded from GA** — treated as particular charges on salved values.

### Correct structure
Attribute salvage remuneration (Art. 14/SCOPIC) to particular charges; not allowed in GA under Rule VI.
